# Polynomial Times, 2026–27 (Issue 2)

> Simons Institute for the Theory of Computing · annual magazine · [the PDF in Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1TgqHhYYN2b-OU5bD9FPZweSB_6HRJ--M/view)

A magazine, so each article is a chapter. Summaries are in our own words; the text stays in the PDF.

## The book in one paragraph

The Simons Institute’s annual magazine, this year centered on how AI is changing theoretical computer science. The lead feature reports a workshop where theorists weighed AI-generated proofs, including resolved long-standing conjectures and the disputed Navier–Stokes announcement, against the view that mathematics exists to build human understanding. The rest covers algorithmic fairness and law, post-quantum security proofs, rapid progress toward fault-tolerant quantum computing with neutral atoms, succinct proofs (SNARGs) for NP, a portrait of a founder of computational biology, and the year’s research programs.

**Contents**

1. [Letter from the Director](ch1): The director introduces an issue focused on AI’s impact on the field and new funding for that work.
2. [Theoretical Computer Science Meets Its AI Moment](ch2): Theorists debate what AI-produced results (Matrix Spencer, Komlós, the contested Navier–Stokes claim) mean for doing mathematics, framed by Thurston’s view that the product of mathematics is understanding, not theorems.
3. [Algorithms, Society, and the Law](ch3): The Institute’s Law and Society fellows and their workshop on AI governance with policymakers and researchers.
4. [A Conversation About Algorithmic Fairness](ch4): Kannan, Kleinberg, Reingold and Vohra discuss how definitions of fairness developed, from fair division to predictive algorithms, why fairness criteria conflict, and how the field relates to law and AI alignment.
5. [A Portrait of Nick Spooner](ch5): Nick Spooner’s work on proving that post-quantum cryptographic proof systems are actually secure.
6. [Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computing with Neutral Atoms](ch6): How neutral-atom hardware, atom transport and more efficient error-correcting codes have brought fault-tolerant quantum computing closer than expected.
7. [The Proof Behind the Proof: SNARGs for NP](ch7): Kalai and Jain explain succinct non-interactive arguments: why NP can’t have short unconditional proofs, how cryptography gets around this, and new routes to SNARGs for NP via proof complexity.
8. [A Portrait of Dan Gusfield](ch8): Dan Gusfield’s role in founding algorithmic computational biology, including perfect phylogeny, and as an expositor.
9. [This Year’s Research Programs](ch9): The 2026–27 programs: pseudorandomness and high-dimensional expansion, spectral theory beyond graphs, and symmetry in constraint satisfaction.
