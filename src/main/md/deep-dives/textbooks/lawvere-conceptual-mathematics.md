> F. William Lawvere and Stephen H. Schanuel · Cambridge University Press, 2nd edition 2009 · [the PDF in Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/12QG-QTJS3xEvn8fwlog55FMwsGzHwMo2/view)

Tests, quizzes and short summary pages between sessions are not broken out. Summaries are in our own words; the text stays in the PDF.

## The book in one paragraph

A gentle first course in category theory that assumes only high-school algebra, built from “Articles” that set out ideas and classroom “Sessions” that work through them with students. Starting from sets and maps, it develops composition, isomorphisms, sections, retractions and idempotents (with Brouwer’s fixed-point theorem as an application), then categories of structured sets such as endomaps, dynamical systems and graphs, universal mapping properties (terminal and initial objects, products, sums, distributive and linear categories), and finally exponentials, cartesian closed categories, subobjects, truth values and toposes.

**Contents**

1. [Session 1. Galileo and multiplication of objects](ch1): Galileo’s analysis of motion as a product of objects, motivating the categorical viewpoint.
2. [Part I: The category of sets](ch2): Sets, maps and composition as the first example of a category.
3. [Part II: The algebra of composition](ch3): The algebra of composition: when maps can be “divided”.
4. [Part III: Categories of structured sets](ch4): Categories whose objects are sets with structure.
5. [Part IV: Elementary universal mapping properties](ch5): Universal mapping properties.
6. [Part V: Higher universal mapping properties](ch6): Exponentials, subobjects, truth values and toposes.
