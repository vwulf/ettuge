# Category Theory for Scientists

> David I. Spivak · arXiv 1302.6946 (old version of the MIT Press book), 2013 · [the PDF in Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1uWXMxetXOQd8QmMZ1OX6ZctMhJCFDXry/view)

The arXiv draft of what became Category Theory for the Sciences. Summaries are in our own words; the text stays in the PDF.

## The book in one paragraph

Spivak introduces category theory to scientists outside mathematics, using ologs (ontology logs: knowledge as labeled boxes and arrows) and database schemas as running examples alongside the usual algebra. It starts with sets and functions, building products, coproducts, limits and colimits concretely in Set, then studies monoids, groups, graphs and orders as categories before naming categories and functors outright. The core chapter covers functors, natural transformations, the equivalence between categories and database schemas, and limits; the last covers adjunctions, functor categories and the Yoneda lemma, monads, and operads as a language for composing systems.

**Contents**

1. [1 Introduction](ch1): A short history, the book’s purpose, and what it asks of readers.
