# A Synopsis of Elementary Results in Pure Mathematics (Carr’s Synopsis)

> G. S. Carr · Francis Hodgson, London, 1886 (scanned copy) · [the PDF in Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/18qqOT2vtrnCgZ6adlnbcnfnrN4Apy26U/view)

A 19th-century compendium of about 6,000 numbered results with abridged proofs, famous as the book from which Srinivasa Ramanujan taught himself mathematics. This is a scan with a poor OCR text layer and no outline, so it is broken down only into its subject sections (located by running headers); article numbers and finer sections are not listed. Summaries are in our own words.

## The book in one paragraph

G. S. Carr compiled this synopsis as a crammer’s reference for Cambridge students: thousands of formulas and theorems stated tersely with brief demonstrations, arranged by subject and numbered for cross-reference, plus a large index to papers in the mathematical journals of the day. Its compressed, proof-sketch style is often credited with shaping Ramanujan’s way of recording results. It spans algebra, theory of equations, plane and spherical trigonometry, Euclidean geometry and conics, differential and integral calculus (with extensive tables of integrals), calculus of variations, differential equations, finite differences, and plane and solid coordinate geometry.
