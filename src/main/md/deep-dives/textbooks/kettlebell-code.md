# The Kettlebell Code (second edition)

> Gregory Dzemaili (Lebe Stark) · e-book · [the PDF in Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1-99cv-5N-zXZ1Kn2WvwjrUMsETrSXUD2/view)

A short training e-book aimed at people over 40. Summaries are in our own words; the text stays in the PDF.

## The book in one paragraph

A kettlebell coach’s case that people over 40 should train with a few versatile movements rather than machines, and his three-part method for doing it. The first part restores mobility and movement patterns (hinge, squat, overhead) with stick drills and light work. The second builds strength with the slow “grinds” (presses, squats, lunges) and power with the ballistic lifts (swings, cleans, snatches), plus loaded carries. The third assembles these into short sessions with warm-ups and recommended weights. A personal prologue and epilogue frame the method as a way of taking charge of one’s health.

**Contents**

1. [Prologue: The Mezzanine of Destiny](ch1): A personal story of a career crisis that led the author to kettlebells, and the argument for short, home-based training over gym machines after 40.
2. [Part I: Loosen Your Body’s Brakes](ch2): Mobility first: drills with a stick and light weights to open the hips, shoulders and spine and learn the basic patterns.
3. [Part II: Turn Force into Form](ch3): The main lifts: grinds (press, goblet squat, reverse lunge), ballistics (swing variants, clean, snatch), and carries.
4. [Part III: Unlock the Code](ch4): Putting it together into sessions: warm-up, activation, main work and finisher, with weights by level.
5. [Epilogue and appendix](ch5): What the book left out (double-bell work and more), encouragement to keep going, and the reference list.
