> Yufei Zhao · Cambridge University Press, 2023 (author’s version) · [the PDF in Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1TU9mMseMt4FaipDfv3OLeNCEjXY0Ix2Z/view)

Exercise lists inside chapters are not broken out. Summaries are in our own words; the text stays in the PDF.

## The book in one paragraph

A graduate text built around the parallels between extremal graph theory and additive combinatorics, organized by the question of when dense structures must contain patterns like triangles or arithmetic progressions. It covers Turán-type problems, the regularity method and removal lemmas, pseudorandom graphs and spectral methods, graph limits (graphons), homomorphism inequalities, Fourier-analytic and polynomial proofs of Roth’s theorem, Freiman’s theorem on sets with small doubling, the sum-product problem, and the Green–Tao theorem via the transference principle.

**Contents**

1. [0 Appetizer: Triangles and Equations](ch1): Schur’s theorem and progressions as a preview of the book’s themes.
2. [1 Forbidding a Subgraph](ch2): Extremal graphs that avoid a given subgraph.
3. [2 Graph Regularity Method](ch3): Szemerédi’s regularity lemma and what it proves.
4. [3 Pseudorandom Graphs](ch4): When do graphs behave like random graphs?.
5. [4 Graph Limits](ch5): Limits of dense graph sequences as graphons.
6. [5 Graph Homomorphism Inequalities](ch6): Inequalities between subgraph densities.
7. [6 Forbidding 3-Term Arithmetic Progressions](ch7): Roth’s theorem via Fourier analysis and the polynomial method.
8. [7 Structure of Set Addition](ch8): Freiman’s theorem: sets with small sumsets are structured.
9. [8 Sum-Product Problem](ch9): A set cannot have both small sumset and small product set.
10. [9 Progressions in Sparse Pseudorandom Sets](ch10): Green–Tao: primes contain long progressions.
