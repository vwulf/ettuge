## The book in one paragraph

The standard reference on the untyped lambda calculus, treated as a theory of functions as rules rather than graphs. The sample shows the plan of the whole book (conversion, reduction, theories and models across five parts) and its opening: why the calculus was invented as a foundation for logic, why that failed because of the paradoxes, and how what survived became the basis of computability and programming-language semantics. It ends inside the first technical topic, complete partial orders and the Scott topology, which give the calculus its models.
