## The book in one paragraph

Yanofsky presents category theory, and monoidal categories in particular, as one language for structures and processes across mathematics, physics and computing. The opening chapter gives the history and motivation: categories began as a bridge between topology and algebra, and monoidal categories add a second way of combining things, so that composition reads as doing one process after another and the tensor as doing two at once. It then lays out the book’s method of many examples plus “mini-courses”, and runs the first mini-course: sets and functions taught so that each construction already hints at its categorical form.

**1.1 Categories**  
Category theory grew from relating geometric objects to algebraic invariants; unification has always been its motive.  
→ PDF pp. 17–19 in [the PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/12diAMZ6y3e96TxobH_uKNfHxEfwEmQCe/view)

**1.2 Monoidal Categories**  
Monoidal categories describe structures and processes; their independence from any one field, once mocked as abstract nonsense, is their strength.  
→ PDF p. 20 in [the PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/12diAMZ6y3e96TxobH_uKNfHxEfwEmQCe/view)

**1.3 The Examples and the Mini-courses**  
Sequential versus parallel composition, the coherence rules relating them, the book’s examples and mini-courses, and notation.  
→ PDF pp. 21–23 in [the PDF](https://drive.google.com/file/d/12diAMZ6y3e96TxobH_uKNfHxEfwEmQCe/view)

**1.4 Mini-course: Sets and Categorical Thinking**  
Sets, functions, products, injections and surjections, and equivalence relations, each introduced with a stated “categorical idea”, chiefly that objects are understood through their morphisms.
