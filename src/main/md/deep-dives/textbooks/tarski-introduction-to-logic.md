# Introduction to Logic and to the Methodology of Deductive Sciences

> Alfred Tarski · Oxford University Press (English edition) · [the PDF in Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1Kq0R8Zk1q39NUtMzO5QAPvkDjS_roRCP/view)

Summaries are in our own words; the text stays in the PDF.

## The book in one paragraph

Tarski’s classic introduction for readers without mathematical training. The first part teaches elementary logic as the language of mathematics: variables and quantifiers, the sentential calculus with truth tables and laws of inference, identity, the calculus of classes, and relations (including functions and order), then explains the deductive method itself: primitive terms, axioms, definitions, proofs, models, consistency, completeness and independence. The second part applies all of this by axiomatizing a small theory of order and addition for numbers, examining its methodological properties, and extending it toward the real numbers.

**Contents**

1. [First Part Elements of Logic. Deductive Method](ch1): Logic as the foundation of deductive reasoning, from variables to the axiomatic method.
2. [Second Part Applications of Logic and Methodology in Constructing Mathematical Theories](ch2): A worked axiomatic theory of numbers and what it shows about the method.
