# The Riddle of Scheherazade and Other Amazing Puzzles, Ancient and Modern

> Raymond M. Smullyan · Alfred A. Knopf, 1997 · [the PDF in Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/12q2ln1GlijDjUZHo-eyPDhrnMSgAFe0G/view)

Each chapter is one entry; puzzles are numbered within chapters and their solutions are collected at the back (PDF pp. 538–686, not broken down). Summaries are in our own words; the text stays in the PDF.

## The book in one paragraph

A puzzle book in two parts. In Book One, Scheherazade, having run out of stories after the thousand and one nights, keeps entertaining the king with riddles, arithmetic and probability puzzles, truth-teller/liar problems about the Mazdaysians and Aharmanites, metapuzzles, and a quest story, ending with the yes-or-no “grand question” that forces the king to spare her. Book Two turns that trick into “coercive logic” and moves on to Smullyan’s signature logic worlds: liars who change by day, ambiguous yes/no machines, disoriented planet-dwellers, split personalities, tricks and games, problems related to Gödel’s theorem, and classic paradoxes of self-reference.
