# Surreal Numbers (opening chapters)

> Donald E. Knuth · Addison-Wesley, 1974 (chapters 1–4) · [the PDF in Drive](https://drive.google.com/file/d/12G5Lfual51qlzhWrhZqmOUaSUCjIhtBh/view)

This PDF holds the first four chapters of Knuth’s novelette. Summaries are in our own words; the text stays in the PDF.

## The book in one paragraph

Two ex-students on a remote beach dig up a stone carved with Conway’s rules for creating numbers, told like a creation story. Working it out together, they rebuild the theory of surreal numbers from two rules: a number is a pair of sets of earlier numbers, left and right, with nothing on the left at least anything on the right; and an order relation defined recursively in terms of those sets. The real subject is how mathematics is done: translating words into symbols, testing conjectures on small cases, generalizing, and finding a proof by trying to disprove a claim.

**Contents**

1. [1. The Rock](ch1): Bill and Alice, months into living off the grid, find a buried stone whose inscription describes Conway creating zero from two empty sets, then one and minus one.
2. [2. Symbols](ch2): Alice rewrites the stone’s rules in symbols: a number is a pair of sets (left, right), and ≤ is defined recursively.
3. [3. Proofs](ch3): They suspect the stone’s “equal” means something weaker, “like”, and start proving small facts about the order, then see a general pattern that covers infinitely many cases.
4. [4. Bad Numbers](ch4): Counting the numbers born on the third day leads them to need transitivity.

## 4. Bad Numbers

*PDF pp. 19–25*

Counting the numbers born on the third day leads them to need transitivity. Trying to disprove it with “bad” triples of numbers, they find that each bad triple yields a simpler one, which is impossible, and so prove the law.
