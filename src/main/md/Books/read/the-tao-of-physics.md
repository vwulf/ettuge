# The Tao of Physics: An Exploration of the Parallels Between Modern Physics and Eastern Mysticism
**Author:** Fritjof Capra | **Published:** 1975



---

## Summary

Fritjof Capra, who trained as a theoretical particle physicist, sets out modern physics (relativity, quantum mechanics and particle theory) for lay readers and compares its picture of reality with Eastern mystical traditions. He presents Hinduism, Buddhism, Chinese thought, Taoism and Zen, and then traces themes he sees in both: the unity of all things, the interplay of opposites, space-time as a continuum, the dynamic nature of matter, and the role of the observer.

Capra relied heavily on the S-matrix "bootstrap" approach to particle physics, in which no particle is fundamental and each is constituted by its relations with the others, and drew a parallel with Buddhist and Hua-yen ideas of interdependence.

The book was a bestseller in many languages and was an early text in the broader genre linking science and spirituality.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Popular influence:** It is credited with introducing many readers to both modern physics and Eastern philosophy, and with launching a genre.
- **Physicists' objections:** Many physicists argue that the parallels are based on loose analogies and that mathematical theories are being read as metaphysics.
- **Outdated physics:** Critics note that the bootstrap model Capra relied on was soon superseded by quark theory and the Standard Model.
- **Selective use of traditions:** Scholars of religion argue the book simplifies diverse Asian traditions into a single "Eastern" view.
- **Holistic vision:** Supporters value it as an argument for a more holistic, less mechanistic worldview.
