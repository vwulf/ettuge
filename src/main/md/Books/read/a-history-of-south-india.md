# A History of South India: From Prehistoric Times to the Fall of Vijayanagar
**Author:** K. A. Nilakanta Sastri | **Published:** 1955



---

## Summary

K. A. Nilakanta Sastri, long a professor at the University of Madras and the author of major studies of the Cholas and other dynasties, produced this survey as an overview of the peninsula's history from prehistory to the fall of Vijayanagara in the 16th century. The book moves through the region's major political powers, from the Sangam age and the Satavahanas to the Pallavas, Chalukyas, Rashtrakutas, Cholas, Hoysalas, Pandyas and Vijayanagara.

Alongside political narrative, Sastri devotes chapters to social and economic life, administration, religion and philosophy, literature in Tamil, Kannada, Telugu and Sanskrit, and art and architecture. It relies heavily on inscriptions, of which Sastri had deep knowledge.

Revised through several editions, it remains the standard starting point for the region's premodern history.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Standard reference:** Historians regard it as a foundational and authoritative survey, built on deep epigraphic knowledge.
- **Dynastic focus:** Later critics note that its emphasis on kings and dynasties leaves less room for social and economic history.
- **Interpretive framework:** Some scholars criticize its tendency to stress Sanskritic and Brahminical influence on South Indian culture.
- **Dense style:** It is comprehensive but can be dry and heavy with names and dates.
- **Dated in parts:** Later archaeology and scholarship have revised aspects of its account, especially for prehistory and the early period.
