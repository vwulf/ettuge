# Best Aikido: The Fundamentals
**Author:** Kisshomaru Ueshiba & Moriteru Ueshiba | **Published:** 2002



---

## Summary

*Best Aikido: The Fundamentals* was written by Kisshomaru Ueshiba, the second Doshu (head) of the Aikikai and son of aikido's founder Morihei Ueshiba, together with his son Moriteru Ueshiba, the third Doshu. It was published in English by Kodansha International in a translation by John Stevens.

The book opens with an account of aikido's philosophy and the founder's intentions, then covers practical foundations: dojo etiquette, posture (kamae), footwork, breakfalls (ukemi) and the basic movements of entering (irimi) and turning (tenkan). The bulk of the book presents fundamental techniques such as ikkyo, iriminage, shihonage and kotegaeshi from standard attacks, with sequential photographs.

Because it comes from the leadership of the Aikikai Foundation, it functions as a statement of the mainstream, headquarters-standard form of aikido basics.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Official standard:** The book is valued as an authoritative reference for Aikikai technique, coming directly from the Ueshiba family lineage.
- **Clear photography:** Readers generally praise the step-by-step photographs as clear and well organized for review alongside dojo training.
- **Limited depth on principles:** Some reviewers note the philosophical sections are brief and that the book is best used as a supplement to instruction rather than a standalone teacher.
- **Limited critical coverage:** As a technical manual, it has received little formal criticism beyond martial-arts readers' reviews.
