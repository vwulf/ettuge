# Matadaana (ಮತದಾನ)
**Author:** S. L. Bhyrappa | **Published:** 1965



---

## Summary

*Matadaana* ('voting') centres on Dr. Shivappa, a medical graduate who returns to serve the people of his village, Rangapura, near Tumkur. Persuaded to stand for election, he is pulled into campaigning, factional manoeuvre and the transactional world of party politics; his medical work suffers, with fatal consequences for a patient he neglects. He loses, and the novel shows how established politicians exploit his idealism and reputation for their own ends, while those who financed the campaign are ruined by its outcome.

Written less than two decades after independence, the novel is an early Kannada examination of what electoral democracy did to village life: caste arithmetic, money, patronage and the corrosion of personal ideals. Bhyrappa keeps the focus on individual conscience and its compromises rather than on ideology.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Political realism:** Critics regard it as one of the more clear-eyed Kannada novels about grassroots electoral politics, notable for showing how idealism is absorbed by the machinery of patronage.
- **Doctor as moral centre:** The choice of a doctor as protagonist sharpens the central conflict between service and ambition, made concrete by the patient who dies while he campaigns.
- **Screen afterlife:** T. N. Seetharam's film adaptation *Mathadana* won the National Film Award for Best Feature Film in Kannada, renewing attention to the novel.
- **Less discussed than the major novels:** It sits among Bhyrappa's early social novels and receives less critical attention than *Vamshavruksha*, *Parva* or *Daatu*.
