# The Dogs of War
**Author:** Frederick Forsyth | **Published:** 1974



---

## Summary

Sir James Manson, a ruthless British mining magnate, learns that a survey has found an enormous platinum deposit in Zangaro, a fictional, impoverished West African state ruled by a brutal dictator. Rather than let the find be exploited openly, Manson plans to install a pliable leader through a coup, and hires the English mercenary Carlo "Cat" Shannon to plan and carry it out.

Most of the novel follows the logistics: setting up shell companies, buying weapons on the black market, acquiring a ship, recruiting a small team of veteran mercenaries, and moving everything into position undetected. The final assault on the presidential palace is brief compared with the long preparation, and Shannon's own aims turn out to differ from his employer's.

The novel draws on Forsyth's experience reporting on the Nigerian Civil War (Biafra) and on mercenaries in Africa. Later press reports linked Forsyth to a planned 1973 coup in Equatorial Guinea, adding to the book's reputation for authenticity. It was filmed in 1980.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Procedural detail:** Reviewers singled out the step-by-step logistics of the coup as the book's main attraction and its most distinctive feature.
- **Neocolonial critique:** Critics note the novel exposes corporate greed and Western exploitation of African resources, even as it glamorizes mercenaries.
- **Depiction of Africa:** Some critics find its portrayal of African politics and people stereotyped and seen mainly through outsiders' eyes.
- **Slow middle:** The long preparatory section is engrossing to some readers and tedious to others.
- **Authenticity legend:** Reports of Forsyth's own connection to a real coup plot have become part of how the book is discussed.
