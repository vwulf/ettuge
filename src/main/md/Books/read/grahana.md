# Grahana (ಗ್ರಹಣ)
**Author:** S. L. Bhyrappa | **Published:** 1972



---

## Summary

*Grahana* ('eclipse') opens with the approach of a solar eclipse in the village of Himagiri: a college principal explains it scientifically while villagers prepare the rituals and taboos that tradition prescribes. At the centre is the swami of the local matha, an ascetic who has urged people to base their lives on values rather than superstition and has built schools and a hospital. His decision to give up sanyasa and marry a doctor sets off opposition from village elders and traditionalists, while other characters face private crises shaped by the same social pressures.

The eclipse works as the governing metaphor, truth temporarily obscured by untruth, and Bhyrappa uses it to examine the relation between belief and reason, institutional religion and personal integrity. The novel is compact compared with his larger works and ends abruptly, leaving several questions open.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Eclipse as metaphor:** Readers note the effective doubling of the astronomical event with the moral one, in which reason and sincerity are 'eclipsed' by custom and public opinion.
- **Questioning institutions:** The sanyasi's choice lets Bhyrappa probe what the ascetic ideal means when it collides with ordinary human needs, a theme he returns to in later novels.
- **Open ending:** A common criticism is that the ending is abrupt and leaves major threads unresolved.
- **Modest critical footprint:** The novel has been steadily reprinted but receives less critical discussion than Bhyrappa's major works; most commentary comes from reviews and surveys of his career.
