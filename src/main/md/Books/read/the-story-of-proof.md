# The Story of Proof: Logic and the History of Mathematics
**Author:** John Stillwell | **Published:** 2022 | **Status:** partially read

---

## Summary

John Stillwell, known for books such as *Mathematics and Its History*, traces in *The Story of Proof* how proof has developed as the core of mathematics. Published by Princeton University Press, the book starts with Greek geometry and Euclid's axiomatic method, then moves through the rise of algebra, the development of calculus and the infinitesimal, number theory, and the discovery of non-Euclidean geometry.

In the later chapters, the narrative reaches the nineteenth- and twentieth-century effort to put mathematics on rigorous foundations: the arithmetization of analysis, set theory and the paradoxes, axiomatic systems, and the results of Gödel and Turing on incompleteness and computability, along with discussion of topology and combinatorics.

Stillwell's thesis is that the standard of proof has repeatedly changed as mathematicians confronted new kinds of objects, and that logic itself became a subject of mathematical study in the process.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Expert synthesis:** Reviewers praised the book as a lucid, authoritative account of how proof evolved, by a mathematician with deep historical knowledge.
- **Real mathematics:** Unlike many popular histories, it works through actual proofs, which critics see as its main strength.
- **Demanding:** Reviewers note that it requires undergraduate-level mathematical maturity and is not light reading for general audiences.
- **Foundations focus:** It is particularly valued for connecting classical mathematics to modern logic and computability.
