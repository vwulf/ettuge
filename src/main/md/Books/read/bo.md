# Bo: Techniques, Forms, and Partner Practices of Ancient Okinawan Fighting Traditions, Vol. 1: Bo-odori
**Author:** Andreas Quast | **Published:** 2024 | **Status:** partially read



---

## Summary

*Bo* is the first volume of Andreas Quast's study of the staff in old Okinawan fighting traditions. Quast, a German researcher and practitioner who writes extensively on Ryukyu martial-arts history (including translations of historical sources), focuses here on bō-odori, the staff dances and partner routines preserved in Okinawan village festivals, alongside related forms such as bō-furi.

The book's premise is that these village and festival traditions, though less well known than the standardized kata of modern kobudō, preserve techniques and practice formats that illuminate how the staff was used and taught. It combines historical background with descriptions of techniques, forms and paired drills drawn from Quast's own years of study.

As a self-published specialist work, it is aimed at kobudō and karate practitioners and at readers interested in Ryukyuan cultural history rather than a general audience.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Limited coverage:** Little published criticism exists; the book is a niche, independently published specialist work.
- **Neglected subject:** Its main contribution is attention to bō-odori and village staff traditions, which are rarely documented in English.
- **Researcher's background:** Quast is known in the field for historically grounded work on Ryukyu martial arts, which lends the book credibility among practitioners.
- **Practitioner focus:** The emphasis on techniques, forms and partner practice makes it more of a study manual than a narrative history.
- **Part of a series:** As a first volume, it covers only part of the planned scope of the staff traditions.
