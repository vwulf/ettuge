# Bubishi: The Classic Manual of Combat
**Author:** Patrick McCarthy (translator) | **Published:** 1995



---

## Summary

The *Bubishi* is a Chinese text on martial arts, medicine and philosophy, associated with Fujian White Crane boxing, that circulated in handwritten copies among Okinawan karate teachers. Masters such as Chojun Miyagi and Kenwa Mabuni drew on it, and it is a key piece of evidence for the Chinese origins of karate.

Patrick McCarthy's translation, first published by Tuttle in 1995 and later reissued as *Bubishi: The Classic Manual of Combat*, presents the text's thirty-two articles alongside a long historical introduction on the transmission of Chinese fighting arts to Okinawa. The contents include the origins of White Crane, fighting strategy and moral precepts, illustrated self-defense techniques, the locations of vital points and the timing of attacks to them, and herbal remedies for injuries.

McCarthy's commentary attempts to connect the text to the kata and applications of modern karate styles, making the book both a historical document and a source for kata interpretation.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Key primary source:** Scholars and practitioners regard the Bubishi as central to understanding karate's Chinese roots, and McCarthy's edition as the standard English version.
- **Valuable introduction:** McCarthy's historical essay on Okinawan and Chinese martial arts is often cited as one of the book's most useful parts.
- **Obscure content:** Critics note that much of the text (vital-point timing, herbal recipes) is archaic, cryptic or unverifiable, and should be read as historical rather than practical.
- **Interpretive layer:** Some reviewers caution that the connections drawn between the text and modern kata reflect the translator's interpretation.
