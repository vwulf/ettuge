# Stick Fighting for Self-Defense: Yawara, Aikido, Cane, Police Club, Quarter-Staff
**Author:** Bruce Tegner



---

## Summary

Bruce Tegner (1928–1985) was an American martial-arts instructor who wrote dozens of inexpensive, widely distributed self-defense books from the 1950s through the 1980s. *Stick Fighting for Self-Defense* applies his approach to sticks of different lengths: the short yawara stick held in the fist, the walking cane, the police club and the quarterstaff.

The book presents basic grips, strikes, blocks and releases with photographs, drawing loosely on jujitsu, aikido and Western stick and staff traditions. Tegner's emphasis throughout his work was on practical, easily learned techniques rather than traditional forms or ritual.

It is a short, illustrated manual typical of Tegner's output, which played a large role in popularizing self-defense instruction in the United States.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Little published criticism exists:** Tegner's books were popular paperbacks and were rarely reviewed formally.
- **Pragmatic approach:** Tegner is credited with promoting simple, non-ritualized self-defense, which some readers value and some traditionalists dismiss.
- **Dated material:** Modern readers often find the presentation and some techniques dated relative to contemporary stick-fighting systems.
- **Historical interest:** The book is of interest as part of the history of how Asian martial arts were adapted for American readers.
