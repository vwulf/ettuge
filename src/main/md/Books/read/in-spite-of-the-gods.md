# In Spite of the Gods: The Strange Rise of Modern India
**Author:** Edward Luce | **Published:** 2006



---

## Summary

Edward Luce, who reported from New Delhi for the *Financial Times*, examines the paradoxes of India in the early 2000s: rapid growth in software and services alongside persistent poverty, a resilient democracy alongside a weak and often corrupt state, and a secular constitution alongside powerful religious and caste politics.

Chapters cover the economy and its reforms, the bureaucracy and the "license Raj" legacy, the role of caste and the rise of lower-caste parties, Hindu nationalism, India's relations with Pakistan, China and the US, and the countryside where most Indians then lived. Luce draws on extensive interviews and reporting.

He concludes that India's rise is real but faces serious obstacles, and that its democratic, pluralist character is both its strength and the source of its complexity.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Balanced portrait:** Reviewers praised it as fair-minded, avoiding both hype and dismissal of India's prospects.
- **Journalistic strength:** Its reporting and interviews give it vivid detail and immediacy.
- **Outsider view:** Some Indian readers found it at times an outsider's generalization, though generally well informed.
- **Dated:** As a snapshot of the 2000s, some of its analysis has been overtaken by later events.
- **Breadth:** Critics note it covers a great deal of ground, sometimes at the expense of depth.
