# Finding Zero: A Mathematician's Odyssey to Uncover the Origins of Numbers
**Author:** Amir D. Aczel | **Published:** 2015



---

## Summary

Amir Aczel, a mathematician and popular science writer, traces his fascination with numbers to his childhood travels with his father, a ship's captain. *Finding Zero* follows his attempt to establish where our place-value numerals and the zero came from, visiting sites in India and Southeast Asia and pursuing the claim, first made by the French scholar George Cœdès in the 1930s, that an inscription from Sambor on the Mekong contained a zero dated to the Saka year 605 (683 CE).

The stone, known as K-127, had been lost amid the turmoil of the Khmer Rouge years; Aczel located it in 2013 in a storehouse of the Angkor Conservation office and campaigned for its protection. Along the way he discusses Brahmi numerals, the Gwalior inscription, Indian mathematicians such as Brahmagupta, and the possible link between zero and Buddhist and Jain concepts of emptiness and infinity.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Detective story:** Reviewers enjoyed the quest narrative and the genuine discovery at its centre, the relocation of the K-127 inscription.
- **Speculative philosophy:** The link between zero and Buddhist emptiness is presented suggestively; critics note it is a hypothesis rather than an established historical finding.
- **Digressions:** Some reviewers found the memoir and travel sections diluted the mathematics and history.
- **Later evidence:** The 2017 radiocarbon dating of the Bakhshali manuscript, published after the book, added a new and contested data point to the story of the earliest zero.
- **Popular history of numerals:** It is valued as an accessible introduction to the history of Hindu-Arabic numerals for general readers.
