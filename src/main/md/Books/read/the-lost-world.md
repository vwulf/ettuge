# The Lost World
**Author:** Michael Crichton | **Published:** 1995



---

## Summary

Several years after the events of *Jurassic Park*, rumors of strange animal carcasses lead the wealthy paleontologist Richard Levine to search for InGen's "Site B" on Isla Sorna, where the dinosaurs were actually bred. Ian Malcolm, who survived the first novel, joins an expedition with field biologist Sarah Harding, engineer Jack Thorne, and others, while two children stow away.

On the island they observe dinosaurs living and behaving in a functioning ecosystem, while a rival team from the biotech company Biosyn, led by Lewis Dodgson, tries to steal eggs. Malcolm uses the setting to speculate about why species go extinct, linking behavior and complexity theory.

The title deliberately echoes Arthur Conan Doyle's 1912 novel *The Lost World*. Crichton wrote the sequel partly in response to the success of the 1993 film, and it in turn was adapted by Spielberg in 1997.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Inferior sequel:** Many reviewers found it more formulaic than *Jurassic Park*, with the plot repeating the first book's structure.
- **Ideas about extinction:** Critics note its more interesting material is the speculation on extinction and dinosaur behavior.
- **Commercial motive:** It was widely seen as written to capitalize on the film's success.
- **Characterization:** Reviewers found the characters thin and the children's roles contrived.
- **Popular success:** Despite mixed reviews, it was a major bestseller.
