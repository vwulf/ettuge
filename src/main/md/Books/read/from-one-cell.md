# From One Cell: A Journey into Life's Origins and the Future of Medicine
**Author:** Ben Stanger | **Published:** 2023 | **Status:** partially read



---

## Summary

Ben Stanger, a gastroenterologist and cell biologist at the University of Pennsylvania, sets out to explain one of biology's central questions: how a single cell divides, differentiates and organizes itself into tissues and organs.

The book proceeds through the history of developmental biology and its key ideas: how cells multiply and specialize, how they communicate, how body plans and organs form, and how the link between genes and cell behavior was discovered. Stanger tells this through biographical sketches of researchers and notes that breakthroughs came from unexpected areas such as radiation poisoning, virus structure and tumor growth.

The final part looks forward to regenerative medicine, including stem cells and organ repair, with possible applications to cancer and degenerative disease, while stressing how far the field still has to go.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Authoritative account:** Kirkus called it an authoritative account of a critical area of medical research.
- **Narrative through scientists:** Reviewers note the use of biographical sketches to make the history of the field engaging.
- **Dense sections:** Some passages are dense and require careful reading, although a glossary and restraint with jargon help.
- **Measured optimism:** Critics credit Stanger for conveying the promise of regenerative medicine without overselling it.
