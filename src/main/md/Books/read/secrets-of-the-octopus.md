# Secrets of the Octopus
**Author:** Sy Montgomery | **Published:** 2024

---

## Summary

*Secrets of the Octopus* was published by National Geographic in 2024 alongside the documentary series produced for National Geographic and Disney+. Montgomery organises the book around what makes octopuses remarkable: a nervous system in which most neurons are in the arms, skin that can change colour and texture almost instantly, the ability to solve problems and use tools, and short lives that end soon after reproduction. She draws on interviews with researchers and on field and aquarium observations of different species.

The book's tone is that of Montgomery's earlier work: curious, affectionate and attentive to the octopus as an individual being rather than only a specimen. Large-format underwater photography carries much of the content, making it as much a visual book as a science text.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Companion volume:** Reviewers treat it primarily as a beautifully produced tie-in to the documentary, valued for its photography and accessible explanations.
- **Montgomery's reputation:** Her earlier *The Soul of an Octopus* (2015), a National Book Award finalist, established her as a leading popular writer on octopuses, and this book is generally seen as a more visual, less personal follow-up.
- **Science communication:** It presents current findings on cephalopod cognition and camouflage clearly for general readers, though without the depth of a specialist account.
- **Animal minds:** The book belongs to a broader wave of popular writing, alongside works such as Peter Godfrey-Smith's *Other Minds*, arguing for taking invertebrate intelligence seriously.
- **Limited critical coverage:** As a gift-format companion book it received less detailed critical review than Montgomery's narrative nonfiction.
