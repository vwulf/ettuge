# The Shepherd
**Author:** Frederick Forsyth | **Published:** 1975



---

## Summary

*The Shepherd* is a novella set on Christmas Eve 1957. The narrator, a young RAF pilot, is flying a de Havilland Vampire jet from his base in West Germany to England for Christmas leave when an electrical failure knocks out his compass and radio. Lost above thick fog over the North Sea and running low on fuel, he follows the emergency procedure of flying triangles to attract attention.

A wartime de Havilland Mosquito appears and leads him down to an airfield in Norfolk that seems to be disused. After landing, the pilot learns things about the field and the Mosquito that suggest his "shepherd" was a pilot who died years before.

The story's appeal lies in Forsyth's precise aviation detail (he served as an RAF pilot) combined with an understated supernatural ending. It has become a Christmas favorite and is often read aloud or broadcast at that season.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Seasonal classic:** It is widely regarded as a minor Christmas classic, frequently republished and adapted for radio.
- **Technical authenticity:** Reviewers note that Forsyth's own RAF experience makes the flying sequences convincing, which grounds the ghostly element.
- **Restraint:** The supernatural is handled with understatement, which critics see as the source of its effect.
- **Slight work:** Its brevity means it is regarded as a pleasant curiosity rather than a major part of Forsyth's output.
- **Nostalgia:** The story evokes the RAF's wartime past and the early jet age, a mood readers often cite.
