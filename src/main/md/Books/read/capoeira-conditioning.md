# Capoeira Conditioning: How to Build Strength, Agility, and Cardiovascular Fitness Using Capoeira Movements
**Author:** Gerard Taylor | **Published:** 2005



---

## Summary

*Capoeira Conditioning* is a practical fitness book by Gerard Taylor, a capoeira practitioner and author of a two-volume history of the art (*Capoeira: The Jogo de Angola from Luanda to Cyberspace*). Rather than teaching capoeira as a game played in the roda, the book isolates its characteristic movements (the swaying ginga, low escapes, kicks, cartwheels and handstand-like inversions) and organizes them into workouts aimed at building strength, agility and endurance.

The book is heavily illustrated with photographs of each movement and sequence, and is arranged progressively, from basic stances and mobility work to more demanding combinations. Taylor frames capoeira's ground-based, acrobatic movement vocabulary as an alternative to gym-based training, emphasizing whole-body coordination, core strength and the flexibility of hips and legs.

It sits in the genre of martial-arts-derived fitness books: it is not a complete guide to capoeira's music, ritual or fighting strategy, but a way of extracting its physical training value for practitioners and non-practitioners alike.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Niche coverage:** Little published criticism exists; the book is known mainly within capoeira and martial-arts fitness circles, and reader reviews are the main record of its reception.
- **Practical format:** Readers generally note the clear photo sequences and progressive structure as the book's strengths for self-directed training.
- **Movement over ritual:** By design it treats capoeira as a conditioning system, so it omits much of the art's music, roda etiquette and cultural context, which Taylor covers in his history volumes.
- **Accessibility caveat:** Some movements (inversions, deep floor work) are demanding, and readers new to capoeira may find instruction from a teacher necessary alongside the book.
