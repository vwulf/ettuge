# The Art of Cycling: Philosophy, Meaning, and a Life on Two Wheels
**Author:** James Hibbard | **Published:** 2021



---

## Summary

*The Art of Cycling* (Quercus, 2021; US edition 2023) is built on three intertwined threads. Hibbard recounts his years as a professional racer in the United States from the 1990s into the 2000s, the circumstances of leaving the sport, and his gradual return to riding for its own sake. A third thread follows a long ride along the West Coast with two former professional teammates, during which they reflect on what racing gave and took from them.

Through these stories Hibbard, who studied philosophy, introduces ideas from Western thinkers, including Nietzsche, Kant and others, to ask why people seek out suffering on the bike, how an athletic identity shapes a life, and what freedom and joy cycling can offer. The focus is on the intellectual and emotional dimension of riding rather than training or tactics.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Philosophical approach:** Reviewers, including road.cc, praised it as an enjoyable, thought-provoking exploration of the mental side of cycling and beyond.
- **Accessible to non-cyclists:** Critics suggest the book's questions about meaning and identity make it readable for those who do not ride.
- **Dense in places:** Some reviewers found the philosophical passages hard going at times, interrupting the narrative.
- **Inside view of pro racing:** His account of the lower tiers of American professional racing offers a less glamorous perspective than most cycling memoirs.
- **Limited critical coverage:** It has been reviewed mainly by cycling media rather than general literary outlets.
