# The Naked Ape: A Zoologist's Study of the Human Animal
**Author:** Desmond Morris | **Published:** 1967



---

## Summary

Desmond Morris, a zoologist known for work on animal behavior and for television, treats *Homo sapiens* as he would any other animal species, the "naked ape" among the 193 species of monkeys and apes. Chapters on origins, sex, rearing, exploration, fighting, feeding, comfort and relations with other animals ask how behaviors seen across human societies might be explained by our evolution from forest-dwelling primates into cooperative hunters.

Morris proposes explanations for features such as human hairlessness, pair-bonding, prolonged childhood and many sexual characteristics, often drawing analogies with other primates. His tone is deliberately detached, presenting familiar human life as if observed from outside.

The book was a sensation, translated into many languages and widely discussed, and it led to sequels such as *The Human Zoo*. It helped prepare the ground for later popular writing on evolution and behavior.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Popularizing impact:** It is credited with bringing ethology and an evolutionary view of human behavior to a mass audience.
- **Speculative explanations:** Scientists have criticized many of its specific hypotheses as untestable or wrong, offered with more confidence than the evidence warranted.
- **Cultural bias:** Critics note that it often generalizes from mid-20th-century Western, especially male, behavior to humanity as a whole.
- **Hunter model:** Its emphasis on male hunting as the driver of human evolution has been challenged by later anthropology.
- **Provocation:** Its frank treatment of sex and religion as animal behavior made it controversial and contributed to its success.
