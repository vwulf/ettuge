# Smoke and Ashes: Opium's Hidden Histories
**Author:** Amitav Ghosh | **Published:** 2023



---

## Summary

Ghosh began researching opium for the Ibis Trilogy (*Sea of Poppies*, *River of Smoke*, *Flood of Fire*) and found the history larger than fiction could hold. *Smoke and Ashes* follows the drug from the poppy fields of Bihar and eastern Uttar Pradesh, where the East India Company ran a state monopoly, to the factories of Patna and Ghazipur, the auction houses of Calcutta, and the smugglers and merchants of Canton, culminating in the Opium Wars that forced China to accept the trade.

Ghosh traces the fortunes built on opium, including American families such as the Delanos and Forbeses, Parsi merchants in Bombay and British trading houses, and links the trade to institutions and cities that still exist. He also argues that opium production impoverished the regions that grew it, contributing to the long underdevelopment of the Gangetic plains, and closes with reflections on today's opioid crisis and on plants as historical actors.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Sweeping thesis:** Reviewers praised the ambition of linking a single commodity to empire, capitalism and present-day inequality, while some found the argument overreaching in places.
- **Personal voice:** The book's mix of memoir, travel and history is seen as engaging, though some critics wanted a more systematic historical structure.
- **Hidden beneficiaries:** Its exposure of American and Indian fortunes built on opium was widely highlighted as a valuable corrective to national myths.
- **Bihar's underdevelopment:** The claim that opium cultivation helped impoverish eastern India drew attention and debate among economic historians.
- **Companion to fiction:** Readers of the Ibis Trilogy value it as the factual backbone of the novels.
