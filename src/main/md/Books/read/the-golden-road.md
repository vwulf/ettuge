# The Golden Road: How Ancient India Transformed the World
**Author:** William Dalrymple | **Published:** 2024 | **Status:** partially read



---

## Summary

In *The Golden Road*, William Dalrymple argues that for over a millennium India was a central exporter of culture, creating what he calls an "Indosphere" of influence across Asia comparable to the Hellenistic or Sinic worlds. The "Golden Road" refers to the trade and pilgrimage routes, especially maritime ones, that linked India with Rome, Southeast Asia, China and the Islamic world.

The book covers Roman trade with south Indian ports, the Mauryan and Gupta periods, the spread of Buddhism to Central Asia and China (including the journeys of Chinese pilgrims such as Xuanzang), the Hindu and Buddhist kingdoms of Southeast Asia culminating in monuments like Angkor Wat and Borobudur, and the transmission of Indian mathematics and astronomy, including the decimal numeral system and zero, to Baghdad and then Europe.

Dalrymple frames this as a corrective to histories that center the Silk Road on China and the West, presenting India as a hub of world history in antiquity.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Narrative skill:** Reviewers broadly praised Dalrymple's vivid storytelling and use of travel accounts and inscriptions.
- **Corrective thesis:** Critics welcomed the emphasis on India's role in ancient global exchange, often underrepresented in popular history.
- **Overstatement concerns:** Some historians argued that the idea of an Indian-centered sphere overstates India's influence or flattens the agency of the cultures that adopted Indian ideas.
- **Political reception:** Commentators noted the book appeared amid debates over Indian nationalism and civilizational history, and that its celebratory framing could be read in different political lights.
