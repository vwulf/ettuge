# Capoeira: A Brazilian Art Form — History, Philosophy, and Practice
**Author:** Bira Almeida (Mestre Acordeon) | **Published:** 1981



---

## Summary

Ubirajara 'Bira' Almeida, known as Mestre Acordeon, trained under Mestre Bimba, creator of Capoeira Regional, in Salvador, Bahia, and later moved to California, where he founded one of the earliest capoeira schools in the United States. *Capoeira: A Brazilian Art Form* was his attempt to explain the art to a non-Brazilian audience: its emergence among enslaved Africans in Brazil, its period of persecution, its legalisation and transformation in the twentieth century through Bimba and Mestre Pastinha, and the distinction between Regional and Angola styles.

The book gives considerable space to the roda, the berimbau and the songs, and to the philosophy of malícia, the cunning and playfulness that make capoeira a dialogue rather than simply a fight. It was later reissued in an expanded edition by North Atlantic Books.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Pioneering text:** It is widely credited as a foundational English-language introduction to capoeira, important for the art's spread in North America.
- **Insider authority:** As a direct student of Mestre Bimba, Almeida writes with first-hand knowledge of Capoeira Regional, which gives the book authority but also a Regional perspective.
- **History and myth:** Later historians of capoeira have revised some of the origin stories common in the art's oral tradition, so parts of the historical account reflect the understanding of its time.
- **Philosophy over technique:** Readers note its emphasis on the culture, music and spirit of capoeira rather than detailed movement instruction.
- **Limited critical coverage:** Beyond practitioners and martial-arts reviewers, it has received little formal criticism.
