# The White Tiger
**Author:** Aravind Adiga | **Published:** 2008



---

## Summary

Over seven nights, Balram Halwai writes to Chinese Premier Wen Jiabao, who is about to visit India, to explain the "truth" about Indian entrepreneurship. Born in Laxmangarh, a village in what he calls the Darkness, Balram is pulled from school to work, and later becomes driver to Ashok, the US-returned son of a local landlord, in Delhi.

Balram describes the servant class as trapped in a Rooster Coop, kept in place by family obligations and fear. Witnessing his employers' corruption and their willingness to sacrifice him, he murders Ashok, steals his money, and reinvents himself as the owner of a taxi business in Bangalore, knowing his family will likely be killed in reprisal.

Adiga's debut won the 2008 Man Booker Prize and was adapted into a 2021 film.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Booker winner:** Its prize brought wide attention, and many reviewers praised its energy, satire and bold narrator.
- **Class critique:** Critics highlight its unsparing portrait of inequality, servitude and corruption beneath India's growth story.
- **Authenticity debate:** Some Indian critics argued that Balram's voice is implausible and that the book caters to Western readers' expectations of Indian poverty.
- **Moral ambiguity:** Balram's murder and lack of remorse invite debate about whether the book condemns or excuses him.
- **Satirical tone:** Its dark comedy is admired, though some find the targets too broadly drawn.
