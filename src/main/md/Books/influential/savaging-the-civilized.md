# Savaging the Civilized: Verrier Elwin, His Tribals, and India
**Author:** Ramachandra Guha | **Published:** 1999



---

## Summary

*Savaging the Civilized* traces the life of Verrier Elwin (1902–1964) from an evangelical upbringing in England and a brilliant career at Oxford to his arrival in India in the late 1920s as a member of a Christian ashram in Pune. Drawn to Gandhi and the nationalist movement, Elwin broke with the church and moved to the forests of central India, where he lived among the Gonds and Baigas, married a Gond woman, and produced a long series of ethnographies including *The Baiga* and *The Muria and Their Ghotul*.

Guha follows Elwin's growing prominence, his conflicts with missionaries, Hindu reformers and academic anthropologists, his advocacy of tribal autonomy against both assimilation and exploitation, and his later life as an Indian citizen and adviser on tribal affairs for the North-East Frontier Agency under Nehru. The book draws heavily on Elwin's letters, papers and the recollections of those who knew him.

Published in 1999, the biography examines Elwin's complicated personal life alongside his ideas, and situates him within larger arguments about tribal identity, development and nationhood in India.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Archival depth:** Reviewers praise Guha's extensive use of letters, diaries and official records to recreate Elwin's world.
- **Biography as intellectual history:** Critics note that it doubles as a history of Indian debates over tribal policy, from isolation and protection to assimilation.
- **Balanced portrait:** It is generally regarded as sympathetic but not uncritical, addressing Elwin's personal conduct and romanticism as well as his achievements.
- **Readability:** Guha's narrative style made a little-known figure accessible to general readers, and the book helped revive interest in Elwin.
- **Debates over Elwin:** Some critics argue that Guha's sympathy for Elwin's advocacy shapes the book's judgments on his anthropological methods and critics.
