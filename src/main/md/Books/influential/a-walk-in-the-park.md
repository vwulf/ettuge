# A Walk in the Park: The True Story of a Spectacular Misadventure in the Grand Canyon
**Author:** Kevin Fedarko | **Published:** 2024

---

## Summary

Kevin Fedarko — a former magazine journalist who had left that career to work as a river guide on the Colorado — agreed to a plan proposed by his close friend, the *National Geographic* photographer Pete McBride: to walk the entire length of the Grand Canyon. McBride promised it would be "a walk in the park." Fedarko had not grasped that people who had done it considered the end-to-end traverse one of the hardest hikes in the world, with no trail for much of the way, 120°F days, near-freezing nights and scarce water.

The book recounts how two underprepared men nearly died, abandoned and restarted the journey, and finally completed it over more than a year with the help of expert canyoneers and members of the canyon's Native nations. Along the way it becomes a deep portrait of the Grand Canyon itself — its geology, history and peoples — and of the threats it faces from development, mining, overuse and a changing climate. Running through it is Fedarko's relationship with his father, who first introduced him to the explorers of the American West.

---

## Critical Takeaways

- **Adventure and portrait:** Reviewers praised it as both a gripping, often comic misadventure and a richly informed portrait of the canyon.
- **Environmental argument by stealth:** Critics note it is not framed as an environmental book but becomes one, making a case for protecting the canyon through the story of the walk.
- **Indigenous voices:** Its attention to the canyon's Native nations and their relationship to the land is singled out as a strength.
- **Length:** Some readers find its 500-plus pages and many digressions demanding, though most reviewers felt the detail rewarded the reader.
- **From the author of The Emerald Mile:** It confirmed Fedarko, whose earlier book told the story of the fastest boat ride through the canyon, as a leading writer on the Grand Canyon.
